Arc flash and NFPA 70E

Available fault currents in artificial intelligence ready facilities can exceed 100,000 amperes. Always work from the site arc flash study.

Points

The site arc flash study defines incident energy, arc flash boundaries, and the required personal protective equipment

Never estimate incident energy in the field. Use the study and the equipment labels

Establish the boundary, wear the specified PPE, and use insulated tools where required

De energize where feasible. Energized work requires a permit and justification

Lockout tagout

Control hazardous energy before servicing equipment. Critical systems are often energized by design.

Points

Identify all energy sources, electrical, mechanical, stored, and fluid

Isolate, lock, and tag each source and verify a zero energy state

Apply personal locks. One worker, one lock

Where equipment cannot be fully de energized, an energized work permit and additional controls apply

Restore only after all personnel and tools are clear and the removal follows procedure

Energized work reality

Critical facilities are frequently energized by design because the load cannot drop.

Points

Energized work requires a permit, a justification that de energizing is not feasible, and management approval

Plan the task, the boundary, the PPE, and a rescue and response plan

A second qualified person and clear communication reduce risk

Stop work if conditions change or the plan no longer fits

Emergency power off

Emergency power off exists to protect life and responders, not for routine control.

Points

Emergency power off removes power quickly to protect life during a fire or shock emergency

Activation drops the protected load. Understand the scope before you ever operate it

Only trained, authorized personnel operate it, and only when the procedure requires

Guard against accidental activation and know the restart procedure

Uptime discipline

Never break redundancy without an approved procedure and awareness of the current redundancy state.

Points

Know the current redundancy state before you start any work

Ask the real question: if I remove this component, do I break redundancy, and what is left

Know the back out plan before you begin, not after something goes wrong

Record what was touched, the redundancy state during the work, and when redundancy was restored
