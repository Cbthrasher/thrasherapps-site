Working on a live plant PLC

Plant PLCs run tracker stow, inverter coordination, breaker interlocks, and grid compliance functions. Clearing faults, forcing I/O, downloading programs, or power cycling a processor on an operating plant can move equipment, drop protection, or violate the interconnection agreement. FieldWatt informs; it never directs anyone to override or force controls on a live plant.

Safe response

Program and force operations are for qualified controls personnel under the site change control process, with the control room and asset owner informed. Field technicians observe, document status LEDs and fault codes, and escalate.

Verify against

Follow the site management of change procedure.

Reading PLC status LEDs

Across brands the pattern holds: a steady RUN or OK light means the processor is executing; a flashing or absent RUN with a solid or flashing FAULT light means a stopped or faulted processor; I/O or COMM lights flag module and network state. Photograph the LED states before anyone touches the cabinet; the pattern at time of failure is diagnostic gold and is lost after a power cycle.

Verify against

LED names and blink codes are model specific. Verify against the installed controller manual.

Major versus minor faults

Most platforms distinguish recoverable minor faults (a low clock battery, a math overflow the program tolerates, a removable module hiccup) from major faults that stop the program (corrupt program, failed hardware, watchdog timeout, incompatible firmware). A minor fault logs and runs on; a major fault stops scanning and drops outputs to their configured safe states, which on a solar plant can mean rows stowing or breakers holding their last state.

Verify against

The fault code tables in the controller manual map each code to recoverable or not.

Common PLC fault causes in the field

The usual suspects at solar plants: communications loss to remote I/O or devices (fiber, media converters, switches); an I/O module fault from moisture, surge, or a wiring short; a dead real time clock battery after years in service (timestamps go wrong, some processors fault on power up); and power supply problems including brownouts from a failing UPS. Environmental stress (heat, dust, condensation in outdoor cabinets) drives most of it.

Verify against

Check cabinet heaters, filters, and door seals during PM visits.

ControlLogix and CompactLogix

Rockwell Logix controllers are the most common plant controller platform at US solar sites. Status LEDs: RUN (solid green running), FORCE (amber when forces exist; forces on a plant controller deserve immediate questions), I/O (green solid healthy, flashing green no I/O configured or some unreachable, red faulted), OK (solid green healthy, solid red hard fault, flashing red recoverable fault or firmware issue). A flashing red OK often clears with a fault clear from the programming software; a solid red OK is hardware. EtherNet IP module LEDs show link and network health separately.

Safe response

Note LED states and escalate. Clearing faults or removing forces is a controls engineer action under change control.

Verify against

Verify blink codes against the installed Logix controller manual.

MicroLogix 1400

The MicroLogix 1400 shows up in tracker network control units, met stations, and small auxiliary panels. It speaks Modbus RTU and TCP, DF1, and DH 485, which makes it a common protocol bridge in older sites. FAULT LED flashing red means a recoverable application fault (often clearable from the LCD menu); solid red means hardware. The onboard LCD can display fault codes and toggle the mode switch without a laptop. Its real time clock battery is a known age related failure.

Safe response

Even on a small controller, clearing faults on live equipment follows the same change control expectations.

Verify against

Verify fault codes against the MicroLogix 1400 user manual.

GE

GE RX3i (PACSystems)

GE (now Emerson) PACSystems RX3i controllers appear in plant control and substation cabinets. The CPU OK LED solid green is healthy; RUN indicates executing; a fault table (viewable in the programming software) splits controller faults from I/O faults with timestamps, which is the fastest way to see what actually happened first. Energy Pack capacitor modules replace batteries on newer CPUs; a failed Energy Pack means the controller loses its program on power loss and boots faulted.

Safe response

Record the fault tables before any power cycle; they are the event history.

Verify against

Verify against the PACSystems RX3i manual for the installed CPU.

Emerson

Emerson Ovation controllers

Ovation is a distributed control system: redundant controller pairs with automatic failover, remote I/O, and an integrated operator and historian environment. At solar plants it often serves as both SCADA and power plant controller. A failover event (backup takes over) is logged system wide and is usually the first clue in a plant behavior mystery. Controller and I/O health lives in the Ovation system status displays rather than on cabinet LEDs alone.

Safe response

Ovation configuration changes are engineering actions on the engineering workstation, under change control.

Verify against

Verify against site Ovation documentation; configurations are heavily site specific.

Siemens

Siemens S7

S7 controllers (S7 300, S7 400, S7 1200, S7 1500) appear at sites with European EPC heritage. LEDs: RUN green executing, STOP amber halted, SF or ERROR red for system fault, BF or a flashing bus LED for Profibus or Profinet communication faults. The diagnostic buffer, read with the TIA Portal or Step 7 software, holds a timestamped event history that survives most faults and is the S7 equivalent of a fault table.

Safe response

Same rule as every platform: observe and document; program and memory operations belong to controls engineering.

Verify against

Verify LED meanings against the installed S7 family manual.
